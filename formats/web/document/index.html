Indagorism

Section 01

Preamble

Indagorism holds that meaning is found in what is real. The natural world, the things around us, and our measurable relationship to both are sufficient ground for existence, happiness, and purpose.

Many traditions rest their meaning on mythology and conjecture. Indagorism rests it on what can be observed, measured, and shared. We do not claim certainty. We claim a method: look closely, measure honestly, and change our minds when the evidence does.

This document founds Indagorism, states what its members hold in common, and describes how it may be revised.

Section 02

The Creed

Reality is knowable, and measurement is how we come to know it.

  1. The world is real. It exists whether or not we understand it, and it rewards attention.
  2. Measurement is our common language. A thing measured can be checked, compared, and shared between any two people.
  3. Belief follows evidence. We hold each belief only as firmly as the evidence allows, and we revise it when the evidence changes.
  4. We belong to what we measure. We are made of the same matter and bound by the same laws as everything around us. That kinship is a source of meaning, not a reduction of it.

Changing one’s mind in response to evidence is the central act of faith in Indagorism. It is honored, never punished.

Section 03

Non-Theism

Indagorism is non-theistic. It proposes no formal God, and the universe is our guidance.

On God

Indagorism neither affirms nor denies a deity. It names none, worships none, and asks no member to do either.

No member is required or encouraged to give up beliefs held outside Indagorism. What a member believes beyond this document is their own.

On origins and destiny

We do not claim to know where we come from or where we are going beyond what scientific evidence shows. Whatever lies past that evidence is a Lacuna: named, kept open, and discussed without conjecture.

On spirit

Indagorism looks for wonder in the universe itself. Attention to what is real is its spiritual practice.

On the meaning of life

Indagorism does not hand down a single meaning of life. Guidance in life comes from reason and from each member’s own beliefs and ideals.

Section 04

The Tenets

Seven tenets turn the creed into daily practice.

1. The Tenet of Ten

Indagorists measure in the metric system and count in base ten, because a single shared standard unites people.

Base ten is the foundation of the number system used worldwide in mathematics, and the metric system extends it to length, mass, volume, and time. Together they form the most unified convention humanity has. A measurement made in one place can be understood in every other.

Ten is also tangible. Our fingers and toes give every child a physical basis for counting, so the youngest members can understand the system with their own hands and build upon it.

Units outside base ten, such as the 24-hour day and the 360-degree circle, are accepted. Wherever a choice exists, Indagorists favor the global standard.

We honor ten as a convention, not as a cosmic truth. Its value lies in what it lets us share.

2. Observation

Look before concluding. Attention to the world is a discipline, practiced daily.

3. Calibration

Check beliefs against evidence on a regular schedule, the way an instrument is checked against a standard.

4. Honesty in measure

Report what was found, including what was inconvenient. Never round a result toward what we hoped for.

5. Uncertainty

Every measurement carries an error bar. State what is not known as plainly as what is.

Questions that science has not yet answered are called Lacunas. A Lacuna is not filled with conjecture. It is named, kept open, and discussed like any other topic.

6. Kinship

People, other living things, and the physical world share one origin and one set of laws. Treat them accordingly.

7. Teaching

Pass the method on. A member who understands something owes an explanation to one who does not, in terms a child can follow.

Section 05

Ethics

Indagorism chooses three moral commitments: wellbeing, honesty, and stewardship.

Science describes what is. It does not, by itself, say what we ought to do. These commitments are therefore chosen openly rather than claimed as discoveries.

  • Wellbeing. Actions are judged by their effect on the flourishing of people and other living things. Where effects can be measured, we measure them.
  • Honesty. We do not deceive others or ourselves. This applies to data, to relationships, and to this document.
  • Stewardship. The natural world is the ground of our meaning. We leave it in better condition than we found it.

When these commitments conflict, members reason it through together, in the open, and record how they decided.

Section 06

Acceptance, Freedom, and Individual Rights

Indagorism is founded on freedom of thought and on every person’s right to be fully human, free from the interference of others.

  • Acceptance. Every person is accepted as they are. No one is asked to become someone else in order to belong.
  • Freedom of thought. Each person is free to think, question, believe, and change their mind. No idea is forbidden from examination, and no person is punished for holding one.
  • Individuality. Individuality is critical. Each life is a single, unrepeatable instance, and its differences are to be protected rather than corrected.
  • The best possible life. The ability to lead the best possible life should be guaranteed universally, to every person and not only to members.

No harm

Indagorism will not support or tolerate actions that harm others in any way.

One person’s freedom does not extend to taking the same freedom from another.

Where we stand

We support these rights for our members, in our communities, across our regions, and globally. We support those who live by this purpose and those who serve it, whether or not they are Indagorists.

Section 07

The Calendar

The Indagorist year is marked by six astronomical observances and three days of measure.

Astronomical dates shift by a day or so each year and are set from published almanac data.

Observance Approximate date What it marks
Perihelion Early January Earth’s closest approach to the Sun
Pi Day March 14 Mathematics as the shared language of measure
March Equinox Around March 20 Day and night in balance; a time of beginnings
Calibration Day May 20 The signing of the Metre Convention in 1875; the annual calibration
June Solstice Around June 21 The Sun’s highest point; a celebration of light and growth
Aphelion Early July Earth’s farthest point from the Sun
September Equinox Around September 22 Balance again; harvest and gratitude
Mole Day October 23 Chemistry and the scale of the very small
December Solstice Around December 21 The longest night; remembrance and the return of light

The two solstices and two equinoxes are the high holidays. Each is observed outdoors where possible, with a direct measurement such as a shadow length at local noon, recorded and compared with prior years.

Section 08

Rituals and Practices

Indagorist rituals mark life’s passages with facts that are both true and moving.

Rites of passage

  • Naming. A new child is welcomed with an account of where their atoms came from: forged in stars, carried by the Earth, now gathered into one person.
  • First Count. A young member counts to ten on their fingers before the community and receives their first measuring instrument.
  • Calibration. Once a year, on Calibration Day, each member names one belief they changed and the evidence that changed it.
  • Union. Two people commit to each other with vows they write themselves, witnessed and recorded.
  • Remembrance. The dead are honored with the fact that nothing of them is lost. Their matter and energy remain in the world, and their influence remains in us.

Regular practices

  • Observation. A daily period of attention to the natural world: the sky, the weather, a plant, a bird. Many keep a field journal.
  • Gathering. Stations gather for educational sessions and open-forum discussions, for observances and holidays, and as needed to address world issues. There is no fixed schedule.
  • Sky nights. The community gathers to watch the night sky at each new moon or notable event.
  • Service. Members contribute to citizen science and stewardship: bird counts, water testing, habitat work, tutoring.

Section 09

The Collective

Indagorism is a collective. It belongs to its members together, and no person, office, or group owns it, speaks for it on their own authority, or decides for it alone.

Roles facilitate

Every role in Indagorism exists to facilitate. Those who hold a role help unify the members, carry the purpose forward, and support the people they serve.

A role carries responsibility, not power. No one who holds a role may dictate to members or make a decision on their behalf. Roles are held for a time, open to anyone, and answerable to the members.

Decisions are shared

Decisions that affect members are made with them, in the open. Anyone affected may take part.

Agreement will rarely be unanimous. That is human, and it is not a failure. In every matter, the aim is common ground: a decision that everyone can accept and live with, even when it is not each member’s first choice.

  • Every view is heard before a decision is made.
  • Disagreement is stated plainly and recorded alongside the decision.
  • No side overrules another. Where common ground has not yet been found, members keep reasoning together until it is.
  • Decisions stay open to revision when evidence or circumstances change.

No one decides alone

No member, role, or group may decide alone for Indagorism. A decision made by one on behalf of all is not a decision of Indagorism.

Section 10

Structure

Indagorism has no prophets and no clergy. Authority rests in evidence and in the review of peers. Every role below exists to facilitate, as set out in The Collective.

  • Members are called Indagorists. Anyone who accepts the creed may join. Membership is open-ended. Its only commitment is acceptance of these ideals and open-mindedness toward the thoughts of others, inside and outside Indagorism. No one is asked to renounce another tradition.
  • Stations are local communities. A station forms when a handful of Indagorists agree to gather.
  • Calibrators are facilitators, one for every ten members. They serve terms of up to ten months, set by their availability, and may be re-elected by the members they serve. They organize gatherings and lead rites. They hold no special access to truth.
  • The Council of Review is made up of calibrators from the stations. It keeps the record of this document and facilitates the review of proposed amendments, as set out in The Living Canon.
  • The Catalyst is one Indagorist appointed by the calibrators to represent Indagorism. The Catalyst speaks for the Indagorists, conveying what members have decided rather than a view of their own, and helps guide the Council of Review in its collaboration and processes. Like a catalyst in chemistry, they help the work along without deciding its outcome. The calibrators set the Catalyst’s term and may reappoint or replace them.
  • Founders are the Indagorists responsible for founding Indagorism. Together they convene the first station and the first Council of Review. Where the law requires named people to act for Indagorism, such as to sign on its behalf, the Founders serve in that role until members name others. They sign only what members have decided, and founding gives them no other authority.

No member, calibrator, Catalyst, founder, or council is exempt from question. A claim stands on its evidence, whoever makes it.

Section 11

The Living Canon

This document is expected to change. Its current version is shown on its title page.

  1. Any Indagorist may propose an amendment in writing, with the reasoning and evidence for it.
  2. The proposal is open to all members for comment and discussion. Reviews are convened as needed and take as long as they need.
  3. The Council of Review facilitates the review. It gathers every view, helps members find common ground, and records any disagreement. It does not decide on the members’ behalf.
  4. A proposal is adopted, revised, or set aside when members reach common ground on it. The Council publishes the outcome and the reasoning behind it.
  5. Adopted changes produce a new numbered version. Every prior version is kept on record.

The creed may be amended like any other part. A canon that cannot be corrected would contradict it.

Section 12

The Emblem

The emblem of Indagorism is an error bar set inside a ring of ten marks.

  • The point at the center is a single measurement.
  • The bar and its two caps are the uncertainty that every honest measurement admits. Together they also form the letter I.
  • The ring of ten marks is the Tenet of Ten, drawn as the dial of an instrument.

A child should be able to draw it from memory.

Meaning is found in what is real, and measurement is how we come to know it.
