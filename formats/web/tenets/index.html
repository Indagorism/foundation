Seven tenets turn the creed into daily practice.

1. The Tenet of Ten

Indagorists measure in the metric system and count in base ten, because a single shared standard unites people.

Base ten is the foundation of the number system used worldwide in mathematics, and the metric system extends it to length, mass, volume, and time. Together they form the most unified convention humanity has. A measurement made in one place can be understood in every other.

Ten is also tangible. Our fingers and toes give every child a physical basis for counting, so the youngest members can understand the system with their own hands and build upon it.

Units outside base ten, such as the 24-hour day and the 360-degree circle, are accepted. Wherever a choice exists, Indagorists favor the global standard.

We honor ten as a convention, not as a cosmic truth. Its value lies in what it lets us share.

2. Observation

Look before concluding. Attention to the world is a discipline, practiced daily.

3. Calibration

Check beliefs against evidence on a regular schedule, the way an instrument is checked against a standard.

4. Honesty in measure

Report what was found, including what was inconvenient. Never round a result toward what we hoped for.

5. Uncertainty

Every measurement carries an error bar. State what is not known as plainly as what is.

Questions that science has not yet answered are called Lacunas. A Lacuna is not filled with conjecture. It is named, kept open, and discussed like any other topic.

6. Kinship

People, other living things, and the physical world share one origin and one set of laws. Treat them accordingly.

7. Teaching

Pass the method on. A member who understands something owes an explanation to one who does not, in terms a child can follow.
