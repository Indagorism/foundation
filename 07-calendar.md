# The Calendar

The Indagorist year is marked by six astronomical observances and three days of measure.

Astronomical dates shift by a day or so each year and are set from published almanac data.

| Observance | Approximate date | What it marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perihelion | Early January | Earth's closest approach to the Sun |
| Pi Day | March 14 | Mathematics as the shared language of measure |
| **March Equinox** | Around March 20 | Day and night in balance; a time of beginnings |
| Calibration Day | May 20 | The signing of the Metre Convention in 1875; the annual calibration |
| **June Solstice** | Around June 21 | The Sun's highest point; a celebration of light and growth |
| Aphelion | Early July | Earth's farthest point from the Sun |
| **September Equinox** | Around September 22 | Balance again; harvest and gratitude |
| Mole Day | October 23 | Chemistry and the scale of the very small |
| **December Solstice** | Around December 21 | The longest night; remembrance and the return of light |

The two solstices and two equinoxes are the high holidays. Each is observed outdoors where possible, with a direct measurement such as a shadow length at local noon, recorded and compared with prior years.
