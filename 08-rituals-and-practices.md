# Rituals and Practices

Indagorist rituals mark life's passages with facts that are both true and moving.

## Rites of passage

- **Naming.** A new child is welcomed with an account of where their atoms came from: forged in stars, carried by the Earth, now gathered into one person.
- **First Count.** A young member counts to ten on their fingers before the community and receives their first measuring instrument.
- **Calibration.** Once a year, on Calibration Day, each member names one belief they changed and the evidence that changed it.
- **Union.** Two people commit to each other with vows they write themselves, witnessed and recorded.
- **Remembrance.** The dead are honored with the fact that nothing of them is lost. Their matter and energy remain in the world, and their influence remains in us.

## Regular practices

- **Observation.** A daily period of attention to the natural world: the sky, the weather, a plant, a bird. Many keep a field journal.
- **Gathering.** Stations gather for educational sessions and open-forum discussions, for observances and holidays, and as needed to address world issues. There is no fixed schedule.
- **Sky nights.** The community gathers to watch the night sky at each new moon or notable event.
- **Service.** Members contribute to citizen science and stewardship: bird counts, water testing, habitat work, tutoring.
